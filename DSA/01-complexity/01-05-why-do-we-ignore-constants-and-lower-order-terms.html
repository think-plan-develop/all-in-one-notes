01.05 - Why do we ignore constants and lower-order terms?

As input size grows, the highest-order term eventually dominates a polynomial expression. Asymptotic analysis focuses on that long-run growth rather than fixed multipliers or smaller terms.

  • 7n + 20 grows linearly, so it is O(n).
  • 3n^2 + 10n + 8 grows quadratically, so it is O(n^2).
  • Multiplying by a fixed constant changes actual runtime, but not the asymptotic growth class.
For n = 10, lower-order terms may still matter in practice. Big-O describes behavior as n becomes large; it does not claim all O(n) algorithms are equally fast for every input size.
Key idea: drop constant factors and lower-order terms when stating asymptotic growth, but consider them when benchmarking real implementations.
