01.03 - Understand O(2n) and O(n!)

Exponential and factorial algorithms can grow very quickly, so even modest increases in input size may multiply the amount of work.

O(2n) - exponential

A common source is making two recursive choices for each of n positions. For example, enumerating all subsets of a set produces 2n subsets.

O(n!) - factorial

Trying every ordering of n distinct items produces n! permutations. The number of possibilities multiplies by one more choice at each position.

Generating all candidates can require exponential or factorial time even if processing each individual candidate is fast. Look for pruning, dynamic programming, or constraints that reduce the search.
Key idea: these approaches are usually suitable only for small inputs unless pruning or special structure substantially reduces the search.
