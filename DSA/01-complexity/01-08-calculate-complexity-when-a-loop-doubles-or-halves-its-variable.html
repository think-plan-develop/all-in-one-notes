01.08 - Calculate complexity when a loop doubles or halves its variable

When a loop changes its variable multiplicatively, count how many multiplications or divisions are needed to reach the stopping condition.

i = 1
while i < n:
    work()
    i = i * 2

After k iterations, i = 2^k. The loop stops when 2^k reaches n, so k is about log2(n): time is O(log n).

Repeatedly halving n until it reaches one also takes O(log n) iterations. The logarithm base is irrelevant in Big-O because changing a constant base only changes a constant factor.

Key idea: additive progress often gives linear iterations; multiplicative progress often gives logarithmic iterations.
